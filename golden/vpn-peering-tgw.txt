VPN (Virtual Private Network) creates an encrypted tunnel between two endpoints over an network.
For example, AWS Site-to-Site VPN securely connects your company's data center to your AWS VPC.
In background, Your company's VPN gateway encrypts outgoing traffic, which travels through the public internet, and the AWS VPN gateway decrypts it.
This allows both networks to communicate securely using their private IP addresses.

VPC Peering: help to create a direct, private connection between two VPCs (Virtual private cloud) 
that too using AWS infra without sending traffic over the public internet.
But there's one important limitation: VPC Peering is not transitive.
If VPC A connects to B, and B connects to C, that doesn't mean A can communicate with C.
You would need another peering connection between A and C.

Now imagine you have 20 VPCs. Connecting these together with VPC Peering will be a mess right.
That's where AWS Transit Gateway comes in. Think of it as a central router.
Instead of connecting every VPC directly to every other, you attach them to a Transit Gateway.
It routes traffic between these networks based on its routing tables. 
It can connect your on-premises datacenter through a AWS Site-to-Site VPN.

So here's the takeaway:
VPN creates an encrypted tunnel between networks.
VPC Peering directly connects two VPCs using private IP addresses, but it don't support transitive routing.
Transit Gateway acts as a central router connecting multiple VPCs and on-premises networks.
